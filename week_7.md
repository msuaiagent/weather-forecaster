## 2. Preparing for the Competition

### 2.1 Format

At the final competition, we will be testing your model's performance on the most recent weather data from the Open-Meteo API. This means that your model will be making predictions on data that it has never seen before (and this will be verified to prevent cheating), which is a great way to test its generalization capabilities. The competition will be based on the RMSE value of your model's temperature predictions, so the goal is to have the lowest RMSE value possible. The competition will be held in a live format, where you will be given the specific API call to make (with room/time to adjust it based off of your model architecture) and your model will make predictions on that data in real-time.

We'll also have a segment where you will present your unique idea for your project and showcase it (if already implemented) in your Streamlit app. The unique idea and its implementation will be a part of the judging criteria, so it's important to have a clear and well-defined idea that can be easily communicated and demonstrated.

### 2.2 Scoring

There is not a strict scoring rubric for the final competition, but the main criteria will be based on the RMSE value of your model's temperature predictions. The lower the RMSE value, the better your model is performing. Additionally, the unique idea and its implementation will also be considered in the judging criteria, and I will be looking for projects that are unique, interesting, and well-executed. The goal is to create a project that is not only accurate, but also creative and engaging!

### 2.3 Tips for Success

- Make sure that you model is working as expected and the best it can be!
- Make sure that you have a well thought out idea for your project that's ready to be communicated clearly!
- Make sure to test your model's autoregressive prediction capabilities like we implemented in Week 5. This will be important for the final competition, as your model will be making predictions on data that it has never seen before using a very similar way.
- Make sure you have a solid understanding of your model's architecture and how it works. This will be important for making any adjustments or improvements to your model during the competition.
- Make sure you are not overfitting your model to the training data. This will be important for ensuring that your model can generalize well to new data and perform well in the competition.
